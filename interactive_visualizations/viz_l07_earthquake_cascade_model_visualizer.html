The Cascade Model of Earthquake Nucleation

In earthquake physics, the Cascade Model posits that large earthquakes originate as ordinary micro-earthquakes (foreshocks) on discrete fault patches. Rather than requiring a large pre-existing slow-slip zone, mainshocks grow via a domino-like static and dynamic stress transfer chain reaction.

Key Cascade Principles:
  1. Discrete Sub-Events (M1, M2, ...): Small locked asperities rupture seismically, dropping local stress ($\Delta \tau$).
  2. Coulomb Stress Transfer ($\Delta \text{CFF}$): Slip on $M_i$ increases shear stress concentration on neighboring locked fault patches.
  3. Progressive Failure Chain: If adjacent patch stress reaches critical strength ($\tau + \Delta \text{CFF} \ge \tau_{\text{crit}}$), it ruptures, generating foreshock $M_{i+1}$.
  4. Runaway Mainshock Breakdown: As progressively larger adjacent patches fail in sequence, total seismic moment $M_0$ escalates rapidly into an uncontained dynamic mainshock.
Cascade Model
  • Driven by discrete seismic foreshocks
  • Stress transfer ($\Delta \text{CFF}$) between patches
  • No fixed pre-slip size required
  • Culminates in dynamic mainshock rupture
Pre-Slip Model
  • Driven by continuous aseismic slow-slip
  • Requires critical patch length Lc ~ sigma_n^-1}$
  • Foreshocks are secondary passive failures
  • Unstable breakdown occurs when L > Lc
