Seismological Pre-Slip Nucleation Model

In modern earthquake physics (Dieterich 1992, Ellsworth & Beroza 1995), dynamic earthquake rupture is preceded by a period of accelerating non-seismic (aseismic) slip localized within a growing fault patch L(t).

Key Nucleation Sequence:
  1. Quasi-static Creep: Tectonic motion loads fault shear stress tau.
  2. Accelerating Aseismic Pre-slip: Localized patch L begins slipping at sub-seismic speeds (V = 10^-6 m/s). Stress concentration at patch margins pushes nearby micro-asperities to fail as foreshocks (r(t) ~ 1/(tc - t)).
  3. Critical Nucleation Size (Lc): Unstable breakdown occurs when patch size reaches Lc. Notice that Lc ~ σn^-1.
  4. Dynamic Rupture Breakout: Slip velocity jumps from 10^-3 m/s to >1 m/s, propagating outwards at shear wave speed Vr ~ 0.9 Vs.
